DICE: Defensive Incident Containment Exercises — an AI-driven tabletop game
for cybersecurity incident-response training, demoed live at [CONFERENCE NAME]
[DATE].

Most IR tabletop exercises are a facilitator, a whiteboard, and a binder of
injects. DICE turns that into something closer to a role-playing game: an LLM
runs the Dungeon Master, narrating a live security incident in real time while
players pick a defender archetype, declare actions, and roll to resolve them
against an adversary that's actively working through its own kill chain.

This walkthrough covers:

🎭 Character creation — defender archetypes, point-buy stats, skills, and
traits, and how they translate into what a character is actually good at
during an incident.

🖥️ Game layout — the session interface: the narrative feed, the scenario
clock, initiative order, and the controls a player and a facilitator each
have during a live exercise.

🎲 Gameplay — a full round of play: declaring an action, rolling against a
DC, the AI DM narrating outcomes and escalating the incident, and the
adversary advancing its kill chain in response.

📋 Hot wash — the after-action report generated the moment a session ends:
decision-by-decision grading, critical hits/fails, injects survived, and
AI-generated feedback on what the team could have done differently.

📊 Analytics reporting — how performance rolls up across sessions over time,
for tracking a team's (or an individual's) growth as they run more exercises.

DICE is built for authorized training use on a trusted local network — a
facilitator hosts a session and players join from their own devices, all on
one machine's LLM API key. It's not exposed to the internet and isn't meant
for operational use.
